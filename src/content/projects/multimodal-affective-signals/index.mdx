Physiological signals are an honest but noisy window into affective state — informative when
aligned correctly across modalities and subjects, and misleading the moment a model finds a shortcut
through subject identity instead of the state it is meant to classify. This project builds a
pipeline that treats both problems, alignment and leakage, as first-class constraints rather than as
preprocessing footnotes.

## Approach

- A **self-supervised contrastive learning framework** (SimCLR/MoCo-style) pretrains on unlabeled
  ECG and GSR windows before any boredom-vs-neutral labels are introduced, reaching **87% held-out
  classification accuracy** across 73 subjects.
- An **ECG-anchored multi-rate alignment pipeline** maps 256Hz ECG to 16Hz GSR with zero temporal
  drift, using the higher-rate ECG stream as the timing reference for the slower GSR channel.
- Signals are denoised with **Daubechies-4 wavelet soft-thresholding** and **Butterworth
  filtering**, via continuous wavelet transform scalograms, before entering the contrastive encoder.
- The full pipeline is tuned with a **200-trial Optuna search**, and **Fisher-Discriminant-Ratio
  gates** (label FDR > 0.15, subject FDR < 0.25) are enforced on the learned representation to
  eliminate cross-subject identity leakage — the failure mode where a classifier learns to recognise
  *who* is wearing the sensor rather than *what state* they are in.

## Why the leakage gate matters

A model that separates subjects instead of states can still report a high held-out accuracy if the
split does not force generalisation across subjects. The FDR gate makes that failure visible during
training rather than after deployment, by directly measuring how much subject identity, versus
label, the representation encodes.
