Patient–clinician communication breaks down in predictable places: a symptom described in everyday
language that maps to several clinical terms, a history that the patient does not know is relevant,
a follow-up question that never gets asked because the consultation ran out of time. The platform —
winner of the [GAVS Technologies Hackathon](/achievements) — targets longitudinal health tracking
rather than a single consultation.

## Approach

The platform treats those as separable problems and gives each one an agent, rather than asking a
single model to hold the whole conversation.

- **LoRA-PEFT tuning** adapts a base model to medical phrasing without the cost — or the
  overfitting risk — of full fine-tuning on a small clinical corpus.
- **Multi-agent coordination** splits intake, clarification, and summarisation, so each step has a
  bounded job and a checkable output instead of one opaque generation.
- **Audio understanding** lets a patient describe a symptom out loud, which is both faster and more
  accurate than asking them to select from a list of terms they may not recognise.

## Design constraint

Everything the platform generates is addressed to a clinician or reviewed by one. That is a
deliberate boundary: the system routes, structures, and summarises information, and does not
deliver a conclusion to a patient on its own.
