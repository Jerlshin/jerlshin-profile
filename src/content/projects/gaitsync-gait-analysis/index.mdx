Multimodal gait analysis lives or dies on synchronization. A video stream and a wearable IMU each
have their own clock, their own sampling rate, and their own drift characteristics, and a gait
event that looks aligned at the start of a recording can be tens of milliseconds off by the end.
GaitSync treats that as a problem to solve in closed form rather than to patch with post-hoc
smoothing.

## Approach

- A **dual-system C² B-spline time-warp engine** models the non-linear drift between the video and
  IMU clocks directly, rather than assuming a fixed offset or a constant frame rate.
- **Quantile transport** and **ground-plane homography** correct the residual non-linear drift
  between the two streams, enforcing closed-form gait phase-identities to **2ms tolerance** across
  **240fps video** and **60Hz IMU** data.
- **ONNX Runtime with CoreML GPU execution**, combined with smart frame decimation, cuts full
  session processing from 3.9 hours to 29 minutes per 15.7-minute recording — an 8x speedup — while
  holding event recall precision at 0.94–0.97.
- A **multi-process Electron/React annotator**, built with zero-copy binary IPC and Web Worker
  Canvas rendering, brings interactive review latency down from 2.26ms to 0.92ms across 4,000
  events spread over 30 channels.

## Why closed-form matters

A learned or heuristic aligner can get the common case right and drift silently on the case that
matters — a limping gait, a missed step, a sensor dropout. Solving the alignment in closed form
means the tolerance the pipeline reports is a guarantee about every session, not an average over
the sessions it happened to see.
