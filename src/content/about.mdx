I'm an M.Sc. Artificial Intelligence student at Friedrich-Alexander-Universität Erlangen-Nürnberg,
with a research focus on multimodal AI, explainable AI (XAI), graph neural networks, and
physiological signal processing. Experienced in integrating physiological, visual, and linguistic
modalities for healthcare and educational AI applications. Driven to advance trustworthy,
transparent, and socially aligned AI.

## What I work on

Most of my work sits at the join between two modalities that were never designed to be read
together — a video stream and a wearable sensor, a physiological trace and a behavioural label, an
image and the clinical vocabulary used to describe it. The interesting problems are almost never in
either modality alone; they are in the alignment, the sampling mismatch, and the question of what a
model is entitled to conclude when the two disagree.

Three commitments run through it:

- **Interpretability as a design constraint, not a report.** If an explanation is reconstructed
  after the fact, it is a description of a saliency method rather than of the model. Choosing a
  representation whose units are already meaningful — regions rather than pixels — is a decision
  taken at the start.
- **Evaluation that can fail.** Aggregate numbers across mismatched regimes hide precisely the
  failure a study should expose. Separating the regimes is usually more informative than improving
  the average.
- **Human-centred, in the specific sense.** The systems I build are used by clinicians, teachers,
  and learners. The relevant question is not whether the model is accurate but whether someone can
  act on its output and be right to.

## Track record

That focus has played out across industry and research labs: architecting **GraphRAG pipelines and
LangGraph multi-agent systems** at Tekaccel Inc.; building **GaitSync**, a closed-form video/IMU
synchronization engine, alongside computer vision work at the **University of Augsburg**; and
pioneering **self-supervised contrastive learning for affective computing** on physiological
signals at **Nanyang Technological University**, Singapore.
