## Scope

Fine-grained visual classification asks a model to separate categories that differ by small,
localised details rather than by overall shape or context. Seed cultivars are a demanding instance
of the problem: the discriminative evidence is texture and margin detail at a scale where intra-class
variation from lighting and orientation is comparable to the inter-class variation being measured.

The architecture is organised into two stages.

### Stage 1 — self-supervised pretraining

A **SwinV2-Tiny** backbone is pretrained with **DINO** self-distillation, so representation
learning does not depend on the size of the labelled cultivar set. Two regularisers keep the
resulting embedding space well-behaved without any labels:

- **Sinkhorn-Knopp centering** balances the teacher output distribution across the batch, avoiding
  the collapse modes that plain centering does not fully prevent.
- **KoLeo regularization** pushes embeddings apart within a batch by penalising small nearest-neighbour
  distances, which keeps the pretrained space spread out rather than clustered.

### Stage 2 — hierarchical mixture-of-experts head

Pretrained embeddings are projected to a 384-dimensional space, $z \in \mathbb{R}^{384}$, and
routed through a **hierarchical mixture-of-experts** classifier:

- **Sparse top-2-of-6 expert routing** allocates capacity per-region of the label space instead of
  sharing it uniformly across visually dissimilar cultivars.
- A **residual FiLM link** conditions the expert pathway on the pooled embedding without discarding
  it, and **cross-attention over spatial grid tokens** lets each expert attend back to the
  backbone's spatial feature map rather than working from a single pooled vector.
- An **ArcFace angular-margin loss** trains the final embedding to separate all 27 fine-grained
  sub-varieties with a fixed angular margin, rather than a plain softmax decision boundary.

### Diagnostics

Because multiple crops can come from the same source photograph, ordinary random splits leak
identity between train and validation. **GroupShuffleSplit** and **StratifiedGroupKFold** are used
throughout the evaluation protocol so that no photograph's crops appear on both sides of a split.
