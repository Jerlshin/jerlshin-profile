## Scope

Hyperspectral seed classification pushes fine-grained classification into a regime where the
useful signal is spread across dozens of narrow, correlated spectral bands rather than three broad
colour channels, and where spatial texture and spectral chemistry carry genuinely different — and
partly redundant — information. SpectralQuadNet is built around keeping those sources of
information separate for as long as possible before fusing them.

### Front-end

A **MaskedSpectralECA** front-end conditions each 40-band cube before it reaches any branch,
applying channel-wise efficient attention while masking out non-informative bands rather than
processing the raw cube uniformly.

### Four non-redundant branches

- **Branch A** — SNV-normalised spectra passed through Savitzky–Golay derivatives, computed via a
  custom **LambdaConv1d** layer, capturing local spectral slope and curvature.
- **Branch B** — 64 soft normalized spectral indices plus continuum-absorption features, capturing
  chemistry-linked ratios that a raw spectrum does not expose directly.
- **Branch C** — a joint 3D+2D spatial CNN, capturing spatial texture across the seed surface at
  two receptive-field scales.
- **Branch D** — **SpecFormer**, a transformer with a wavelength-aware attention bias, capturing
  long-range dependencies across the spectral axis that convolutional branches cannot.

A compact **morphology token** — 8 seed-level shape metrics — is fused alongside the four learned
branches, giving the model direct access to size and shape cues that are cheap to compute and easy
to leak through the spectral branches otherwise.

### Fusion and classification head

The four branches and the morphology token are combined with a **gated low-rank bilinear fusion**
layer (rank-128, 5.19M parameters), which lets branches interact multiplicatively without the
parameter cost of a full bilinear layer. Classification uses an **adaptive sub-centre ArcFace**
head ($K = 3$ sub-centres per class), which tolerates intra-class variation — different seed
orientations and staining — better than a single-centre angular margin.
