## Scope

A convolutional model that classifies a dermoscopic image produces a saliency map over pixels. A
clinician reasons over regions — a border segment, an area of irregular pigmentation. The gap
between those two units of explanation is the practical reason post-hoc attribution on medical
imaging so often fails to convince the people it is meant to convince.

The framework here changes the unit of computation to match the unit of explanation:

- Each image is over-segmented into **superpixels**, which are then treated as graph nodes with
  edges encoding spatial adjacency and appearance similarity.
- A **graph attention** backbone operates on that representation, so learned attention weights are
  already region-level quantities rather than something reconstructed after the fact.
- The head is **multi-task**, predicting diagnostic label alongside dermoscopic attributes, which
  constrains the shared representation toward features that are independently reported by
  clinicians.
