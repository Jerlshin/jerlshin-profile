## Scope

A convolutional model that classifies a dermoscopic image produces a saliency map over pixels. A
clinician reasons over regions — a border segment, an area of irregular pigmentation. The gap
between those two units of explanation is the practical reason post-hoc attribution on medical
imaging so often fails to convince the people it is meant to convince.

The framework changes the unit of computation to match the unit of explanation:

- Each image is over-segmented into **superpixels**, which are then treated as graph nodes with
  edges encoding spatial adjacency and appearance similarity.
- A **Swin Transformer** backbone supplies the per-node representation, so each superpixel carries
  hierarchical visual context rather than raw colour statistics.
- A **graph attention network (GAT)** propagates across that representation, so learned attention
  weights are already region-level quantities rather than something reconstructed after the fact.
- The head is **multi-task**, jointly predicting the diagnostic label and dermoscopic clinical
  attributes on the PH2 dataset, which constrains the shared representation toward features
  independently reported by clinicians.
